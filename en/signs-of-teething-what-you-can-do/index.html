Signs of Teething in Babies and What You Can Do

The short answer: Common signs of teething can include more drooling, chewing on objects, tender-looking gums, and changes in mood or sleep. These signs can have other causes, too, so contact your pediatrician if you have a concern, or if your baby has a fever, unusual crying, a rash, or refuses food or drink. Comfort measures such as a clean teether and gentle gum massage may help make the moment easier.

What are common signs of teething in babies?

Teething can look different from one baby to another. You might notice your baby drooling more, wanting to chew or bite, or seeming unsettled. The gums may look tender or slightly swollen where a tooth is coming through. Some babies also have changes in their usual sleep or eating routines.

These signs are not a sure way to tell what is going on. Babies explore with their mouths for many reasons, and fussiness or disrupted sleep can have causes unrelated to teething. Pay attention to how your baby is doing overall, and ask a pediatrician if something worries you.

  • More drooling: Keep your baby comfortable and change damp clothing as needed.
  • Chewing or biting: Offer a clean, age-appropriate teether while staying close.
  • Gum changes: Look gently, without pressing hard or trying to make a tooth come through.
  • Changes in mood, sleep, or feeding: Notice what is different and seek advice if the change concerns you.

When should I call a pediatrician?

Contact your pediatrician for any concern, and especially if your baby has a fever, unusual crying, a rash, or refuses to eat or drink. These signs should not automatically be attributed to teething. A pediatrician can help you decide what may be causing them and what to do next.

Also seek advice if symptoms seem severe, persist, or are getting worse, or if your baby seems unlike themselves. You do not need to wait until you are certain that something is wrong before asking for guidance.

How can I comfort a teething baby?

Try simple, supervised comfort measures. A clean teether gives your baby something suitable to bite or hold. You can also gently massage the gums with a clean finger if your baby accepts it. Keep the approach calm and stop if your baby seems uncomfortable.

  • Stay with your baby while they use a teether, and check it for damage.
  • Clean teethers as directed for the item.
  • Avoid frozen-solid or liquid-filled teethers.
  • Avoid amber or other teething necklaces because they pose strangulation and choking risks.
  • Do not use teething gels or medicines without first asking a pediatrician.

Can teething cause a fever or a rash?

Do not assume that a fever or rash is due to teething. Contact your pediatrician if your baby has either, or if you are concerned about any other symptom. The same applies if your baby is unusually distressed or refuses food or drink.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my baby is teething?

Drooling, chewing, tender-looking gums, or changes in mood may occur. These signs are not conclusive, so ask your pediatrician if you are concerned.

Can teething affect my baby’s sleep?

Some babies have changes in their usual sleep routine. If the change worries you or comes with other concerning signs, contact your pediatrician.

Should I use teething gel?

Do not use teething gels or medicines without pediatrician advice. A clean teether or gentle gum massage may be alternatives to try while supervising your baby.

Is a teething necklace safe?

No. Avoid amber and other teething necklaces because they pose strangulation and choking risks.
